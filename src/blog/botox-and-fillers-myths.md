Few treatments attract as many rumours as Botox and dermal fillers. Some people worry they will end up with a frozen face; others assume the injections are so simple that anyone can give them. The truth sits somewhere more sensible, and knowing it helps you make a calm, informed choice.

Below, Dr. Junaid Fateh works through the myths he hears most often, and what the evidence and good practice actually say.

## First, the basics: Botox and fillers are different

Before the myths, it helps to know what each treatment does.

| | Botox | Dermal fillers |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A purified protein (botulinum toxin) used in tiny doses | Usually a gel made from hyaluronic acid, a substance found naturally in skin |
| How it works | Relaxes the muscles that cause expression lines | Adds volume, support or hydration beneath the skin |
| Best for | Forehead lines, frown lines, crow's feet | Lips, cheeks, under-eye hollows, jawline, chin, deeper folds |
| When you see results | Starts within a few days, full effect around two weeks | Straight away, settling over one to two weeks |
| How long it lasts | Around 3-6 months | Around 6-18 months, depending on area and product |
| Reversible? | No, but it wears off gradually | Hyaluronic acid fillers can be dissolved |

## Myth 1: "Botox and fillers are basically the same thing"

**Fact:** They work in completely different ways. [Botox](/procedures/botox/) softens lines caused by movement, such as frowning or raising your eyebrows. [Dermal fillers](/procedures/dermal-fillers/) restore lost volume or add shape. Many people benefit from one, some from both, and the right choice depends on what is actually causing the change you see in the mirror.

## Myth 2: "Botox will freeze my face"

**Fact:** A frozen, expressionless look comes from too high a dose or poor placement, not from the treatment itself. A careful approach uses the right amount in the right muscles, so lines soften while you can still smile, frown slightly and look like yourself. That is why starting conservatively is usually wiser. A small top-up at a review is easy; undoing an overdone result means waiting for it to wear off.

## Myth 3: "Botox is a poison, so it must be dangerous"

**Fact:** Botulinum toxin is a powerful substance in large amounts, but medical products contain tiny, carefully measured doses. It has been used in medicine for many years, including for eye-muscle problems, excessive sweating and some types of muscle spasm.

That does not mean it is risk-free. Possible side effects include bruising, headache and, less commonly, a temporary droop of the eyelid or eyebrow. It is not given during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or to people with certain nerve and muscle conditions. Dr. Junaid checks your medical history before treatment.

## Myth 4: "Once you start, you can never stop"

**Fact:** You can stop whenever you like. When Botox wears off, the muscles gradually regain their normal movement and your lines return to how they would have been. They do not suddenly become worse than before. Fillers are gradually broken down by the body in the same way.

## Myth 5: "Fillers are permanent"

**Fact:** Most facial fillers used today are temporary. Hyaluronic acid fillers are slowly absorbed, which is why results last months rather than years. This is actually a safety advantage: if the result is not right, or a problem occurs, these fillers can usually be dissolved with an enzyme.

Permanent fillers do exist, but problems with them can be much harder to correct. Ask exactly which product is being used before any treatment.

## Myth 6: "Fillers always look fake"

**Fact:** The overfilled lips and puffy cheeks you see online come from too much product, the wrong product or poor technique. Well-placed filler often goes unnoticed. People simply look rested or refreshed. Subtle changes, such as restoring a little cheek support or balancing a chin, can make a bigger difference than adding volume to the lips. Filler can even reshape the nose without surgery in suitable people, as with [non-surgical rhinoplasty](/procedures/non-surgical-rhinoplasty/).

## Myth 7: "It's only an injection, so anyone can do it"

**Fact:** These are medical procedures. The most serious risk with fillers, though rare, is the product blocking a blood vessel, which can damage the skin and, very rarely, affect eyesight. Recognising this early and treating it straight away needs medical training and a sound knowledge of facial anatomy.

In Pakistan, injections are sometimes offered in salons, at home visits or by people without medical qualifications, often with products of unclear origin. Before any treatment, ask:

- Who will be injecting, and what is their medical qualification?
- Which product is being used, and can I see the sealed box?
- What happens if there is a complication, and who will I contact?

## Myth 8: "Results are instant, so I can have it the day before my wedding"

**Fact:** Fillers show a change straight away, but swelling and occasional bruising can take one to two weeks to settle. Botox takes a few days to begin working and around two weeks for the full effect. For a wedding, Eid or another big occasion, plan treatment about two to four weeks ahead, and never try a new treatment for the first time just before an event.

## Myth 9: "These treatments are only for older women"

**Fact:** Men have Botox and fillers too, for example to soften frown lines or strengthen a weak jawline or chin. As for age, there is no single right time to start. The decision should be based on your face, skin and goals rather than your birth year. Sometimes the honest advice is that you do not need treatment yet.

Botox relaxes the small muscles that cause expression lines, such as forehead and frown lines, while dermal fillers add volume or shape beneath the skin, for example in the lips, cheeks or under-eye hollows. Botox usually lasts around three to six months and fillers around six to eighteen months, and many people benefit from one, the other, or both together.

Both are quick clinic treatments, which is why they are often confused. In this guide, Dr. Junaid Fateh explains how each one works, which areas suit which treatment, and what to consider before you book.

## Botox vs fillers at a glance

| | Botox | Dermal fillers |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A purified protein (botulinum toxin) used in tiny, measured doses | Usually a soft gel made from hyaluronic acid, a substance your skin already contains |
| How it works | Relaxes the muscles that fold the skin into lines | Fills, lifts or shapes the area where it is placed |
| Treats | Lines that appear when you move your face | Volume loss, hollows, deeper folds and areas you want to shape |
| Common areas | Forehead, frown lines, crow's feet | Lips, cheeks, under-eyes, smile lines, chin, jawline |
| When you see results | Starts in 3–5 days, full effect around 2 weeks | Straight away, settling over 1–2 weeks |
| How long it lasts | Around 3–6 months | Around 6–18 months, depending on area and product |
| Downtime | Usually none | Mild swelling or bruising for a few days |
| Reversible? | No, but it wears off gradually | Hyaluronic acid fillers can be dissolved |

## How Botox works

Every time you frown, squint or raise your eyebrows, small facial muscles contract and crease the skin above them. Over the years, those creases can become lines that stay even when your face is at rest.

[Botox](/procedures/botox/) reduces the signal from the nerve to the muscle, so the muscle relaxes and the skin over it smooths out. It does not fill anything. Common areas include:

- **Frown lines** between the eyebrows
- **Forehead lines** that run across the brow
- **Crow's feet** at the outer corners of the eyes
- **Jaw slimming**, by relaxing an enlarged chewing muscle
- **Heavy underarm sweating**, a medical use many people find helpful in the Pakistani summer

## How fillers work

As we age, the face gradually loses fat, bone support and moisture in the skin. Cheeks flatten, hollows form under the eyes and folds deepen around the nose and mouth.

[Dermal fillers](/procedures/dermal-fillers/) are placed beneath the skin to replace some of that lost volume, or to add shape where you would like more definition. Common areas include:

- **Lips**, for shape, definition or subtle volume
- **Cheeks**, to restore support to the midface
- **Under-eye hollows**, in carefully selected people
- **Smile lines and marionette lines** around the mouth
- **Chin and jawline**, for better balance in profile
- **The nose**, as a [non-surgical rhinoplasty](/procedures/non-surgical-rhinoplasty/) to smooth a small bump in suitable cases

## A simple test: movement lines or resting lines?

Look in the mirror with a relaxed face, then frown and raise your eyebrows.

- **Lines that appear mainly when you move** usually respond well to Botox.
- **Lines, hollows or folds that are there at rest** often point to volume loss, where filler may help.
- **Deep lines present both at rest and with movement** sometimes need both: Botox to relax the muscle and a small amount of filler to soften the crease.

This is only a rough guide. A deep fold beside the mouth, for example, can be caused by loss of cheek support higher up, so Dr. Junaid looks at your whole face, not just the line that bothers you.

## Can you combine Botox and fillers?

Yes, and it is very common. Many faces show both movement lines on the upper face and some volume loss in the mid and lower face. Treating both in a balanced way often looks more natural than using a lot of one treatment.

They can usually be done in the same appointment. Sometimes Dr. Junaid suggests staging them, so each result can be judged on its own and any fine-tuning is easier.

## Safety and side effects

Both treatments are generally safe when given by a qualified doctor using genuine products, but neither is risk-free.

**Botox:** possible effects include small bruises, a mild headache and, less commonly, a temporary heavy eyelid or eyebrow. It is not given during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or to people with certain nerve and muscle conditions.

**Fillers:** swelling, tenderness and bruising are common for a few days, and small lumps can occur. A rare but serious risk is filler blocking a blood vessel, which can damage the skin and, very rarely, affect eyesight. Recognising and treating this quickly needs medical training.

Get urgent help after filler if you notice severe or increasing pain, skin that turns pale or dusky, or any change in your vision. Before any treatment, ask who is injecting, what their medical qualification is and which product is being used. ## Planning around Eid and weddings

Botox takes up to two weeks to reach its full effect, and filler swelling can take one to two weeks to settle. For a wedding, Eid or another big occasion, plan treatment around two to four weeks ahead. Avoid trying anything new for the first time in the days before an event.

## What neither treatment can do

Botox and fillers cannot lift loose, sagging skin or remove excess skin. Overfilling to make up for sagging often leads to an unnatural look. If skin laxity is your main concern, Dr. Junaid may discuss options such as a [thread lift](/procedures/thread-lift/) or a [facelift](/procedures/facelift/), or tell you honestly that injections alone are not the right answer.

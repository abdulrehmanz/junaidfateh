Most men return to desk work within about a week of gynecomastia surgery, wear a compression vest for around four to six weeks, and reintroduce chest workouts gradually from about four to six weeks once cleared. Swelling settles over three to six months, when the final chest shape becomes clear, and scars keep fading for a year or more.

The timings below are typical ranges, not fixed rules. Everyone heals at a different pace, and Dr. Junaid Fateh's team will give you written instructions for your own operation. Always follow those over any general guide.

## Before surgery: prepare your recovery space

A little preparation makes the first week far easier.

- **Two compression vests,** so you can wash one and wear the other. This matters in the Lahore heat.
- **Loose, button-front shirts or kurtas,** so you do not need to lift your arms over your head.
- **Someone to take you home** and, ideally, stay with you for the first night.
- **Extra pillows** to keep you comfortable sleeping on your back.
- **Medicines and dressings** collected in advance, with a plan for when to take each one.

## Days 1 to 3: rest and the compression vest

Your chest will feel tight, bruised and sore, a little like an intense workout. Pain is usually manageable with the medicines you are given. The compression vest is usually put on at the end of the operation and is worn day and night, coming off only when the team says you may shower or change it.

- Walk around the house several times a day to keep your circulation moving.
- Sleep on your back, with your upper body slightly raised.
- Avoid lifting your arms high, pushing yourself up with your arms, or carrying anything heavy.

## The first week: back on your feet

Bruising and swelling begin to fade, although your chest may look swollen and uneven at this stage. That is normal. Many men return to office or desk work towards the end of the first week. You are usually seen for a wound check around this time.

Driving is generally fine once you can turn and move your arms freely, react quickly and are no longer taking strong painkillers. Ask the team before you get behind the wheel.

## Drains, if you have them

Not every gynecomastia operation needs drains. They are more likely after larger reductions or skin removal. A drain is a thin tube that carries away fluid that would otherwise collect under the skin.

If you have one, you will be shown how to empty it and record the amount. Drains are usually removed in clinic within the first few days to a week, once the fluid has dropped. Removal is quick and usually only mildly uncomfortable.

## Weeks 2 and 3: daily life returns

Most everyday tasks feel comfortable again. You can usually take longer walks, and light lower-body exercise may restart if you feel well and the team agrees. Keep wearing the vest and continue to avoid:

- Heavy lifting, including shopping bags and children
- Running and jumping
- Push-ups, bench press and other chest or arm exercises

If your job involves manual labour, lifting or long hours outdoors in the heat, plan for at least two to three weeks off, sometimes longer.

## Weeks 4 to 6: easing back into the gym

This is usually when light gym sessions restart, beginning with low weights and building up over the following weeks. Chest training is added last, once Dr. Junaid is happy with your healing. Stop any exercise that causes pain, pulling at the scars or new swelling.

The compression vest is normally stopped around this time. Some men are advised to wear it for a little longer, for example during exercise.

## Months 2 to 6: the shape settles

The remaining swelling fades slowly, and your chest gradually looks flatter and more defined. Small areas of firmness or lumps under the skin are common and usually soften with time. The nipples and surrounding skin may feel numb or extra sensitive. Feeling usually improves over several months, although it can take longer.

## Up to a year and beyond: scars

Scars around the edge of the areola, or small entry points from [liposuction](/procedures/liposuction/), often look pink at first and then fade over twelve to eighteen months. To help them heal well:

- Keep scars out of direct sun and use sunscreen once the skin has fully closed.
- Start scar massage or silicone products only when the team says it is safe.
- Avoid smoking, which slows healing.

## Warning signs: contact the team

Like any surgery, gynecomastia surgery carries risks, including bleeding, fluid collection, infection, changes in nipple sensation and uneven contour. Dr. Junaid goes through these with you at consultation. Message the team on WhatsApp, or seek urgent care, if you notice:

- Sudden, tense or painful swelling on one side of the chest
- Bleeding that soaks through dressings
- Fever, spreading redness, warmth, pus or a bad smell from a wound
- A soft, fluid-filled swelling that keeps growing
- Calf pain or swelling, chest pain or breathlessness, which need emergency care straight away

## Keeping your result

Breast tissue that has been removed does not grow back. However, significant weight gain, anabolic steroids and some medicines can cause new tissue to develop. Keeping a stable weight helps protect your result.

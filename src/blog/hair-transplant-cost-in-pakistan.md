The cost of a hair transplant in Pakistan depends mainly on how many grafts you need, which technique is used, how much of the work the surgeon does personally, the standard of the facility and what aftercare is included. Because these vary so much from one person to the next, an honest quote can only be given after your scalp has been assessed, in person or through an online consultation with photos.

Dr. Junaid Fateh, an FCPS plastic surgeon who sees hair transplant patients in Lahore, explains each factor below so you can compare quotes properly and avoid the common traps.

## 1. The number of grafts you need

A graft is a follicular unit: a small natural bundle of one to four hairs. The number of grafts you need is the single biggest driver of cost, because more grafts mean a longer day and more work for the whole team.

Your graft count depends on:

- **The area to be covered:** the hairline and temples alone need far fewer grafts than the hairline, mid-scalp and crown together.
- **How far your hair loss has progressed:** early recession at the temples is a very different job from a bald top with only a horseshoe of hair left at the sides.
- **Your hair character:** thick, curly or dark hair can give more visible coverage per graft than fine, straight hair.
- **Your donor area:** the back and sides of the scalp hold a limited supply. A safe plan never takes more than your donor area can spare.

A bigger number is not automatically better value. Taking too many grafts can leave the back of the head thin and patchy. The [hair transplant page](/hair-transplant/) has a graft estimator that gives a rough starting figure, and the real number is set at examination.

## 2. The technique: FUE, FUT, Sapphire FUE and DHI

- **FUE:** grafts are removed one at a time with a small punch. It leaves tiny dot scars and takes longer for large sessions.
- **FUT:** a thin strip of scalp is removed and divided into grafts, then the wound is stitched. It can collect a large number of grafts efficiently and leaves one fine line.
- **Sapphire FUE:** a form of FUE in which the openings for the grafts are made with fine sapphire blades. It changes the instruments used for the openings rather than the way grafts are collected.
- **DHI:** grafts are loaded into a pen-like implanter and placed directly. It gives close control over angle and depth, but it is slower and needs more handling.

A technique name on its own says little about quality. Ask why a particular method is being suggested for your scalp and what it adds in your case. For a fuller comparison of the two main methods, read [FUE vs FUT hair transplant](/blog/fue-vs-fut-hair-transplant/).

## 3. Who does the work

A hair transplant is a long procedure carried out by a team. What matters is who does the key steps: examining you, designing the hairline, deciding the graft count, making the openings and supervising placement. Some low-cost set-ups rely heavily on technicians, with a doctor present only briefly.

With Dr. Junaid, he assesses you, designs your hairline and plans where every group of grafts will go, supported by his trained clinical team throughout the day. Always ask any clinic who will be doing each part of your procedure.

## 4. The facility and safety standards

A safe procedure needs a clean, properly equipped room, sterilised instruments, single-use items where appropriate, careful use of local anaesthetic and staff who can respond if something goes wrong. These standards cost money to maintain. They also protect you from infection and other avoidable problems, so they are worth asking about.

## 5. Aftercare and follow-up

Check what your quote includes after the day of surgery:

- Medicines and any shampoo or lotion for the first weeks
- Help with your first wash and dressing changes
- Follow-up visits, and a review around the one-year mark
- A way to contact the team if you are worried
- PRP sessions, if they are advised, which are often priced separately

## 6. One session or more

If your hair loss is extensive, the work may be split into two sessions several months apart to protect graft quality and the donor area. If you are young and still losing hair, you may want more work in the future. Ask whether your quote covers one session and what the likely long-term plan looks like.

## Why per-graft quotes vary so much

Per-graft pricing looks simple, but two quotes with similar figures can describe very different things:

- **Grafts or hairs:** some quotes count individual hairs. Because each graft holds one to four hairs, a hair count sounds larger.
- **Estimated or actual:** a quote made from photos is an estimate. Ask how any difference on the day is handled.
- **What is included:** consultation, blood tests, medicines, PRP and follow-up may or may not be part of the price.
- **"Unlimited grafts" packages:** your donor supply is finite, so "unlimited" is a sales phrase, not a medical plan.
- **Total versus per graft:** a low per-graft figure on an inflated graft count can cost more overall.

## Red flags in very cheap offers

Be cautious if you notice:

- A fixed price before anyone has seen your scalp
- Very large graft counts offered regardless of your donor area
- No clear answer about who will operate
- No questions about your health, medicines or previous treatments
- Pressure to pay a deposit quickly or a limited-time offer
- Promises of perfect density or a result that cannot fail
- No written aftercare plan or follow-up

Problems such as an unnatural hairline or an over-harvested donor area can be difficult and expensive to improve, and some damage cannot be fully undone.

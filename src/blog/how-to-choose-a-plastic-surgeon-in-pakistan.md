Cosmetic surgery has never been easier to find in Pakistan. Advertisements fill social media, clinics open on every main road, and special offers arrive on WhatsApp. That makes choosing the right surgeon more important, not less. The person who operates on you will affect your safety, your result and how any problems are handled.

Dr. Junaid Fateh wrote this checklist to help you make that choice with confidence, whoever you decide to see.

## Step 1: Check PMDC registration

Every doctor practising in Pakistan must be registered with the **Pakistan Medical and Dental Council (PMDC)**. Registration confirms that the person is a qualified doctor who is allowed to practise.

- Ask the surgeon or clinic for the doctor's PMDC registration number.
- Look the doctor up using the public search on the PMDC website.
- Check that the name matches and the registration is current.

A genuine doctor will be happy to share this. If a clinic avoids the question, treat that as a warning sign.

## Step 2: Check specialist training in plastic surgery

Being a registered doctor is the minimum. For surgery, you also want someone with specialist training in plastic surgery.

Titles such as "cosmetic surgeon", "aesthetic specialist" or "hair transplant expert" do not, on their own, tell you what training someone has had. Instead, ask:

- **What is your specialist qualification?** In Pakistan, a common one is **FCPS in Plastic Surgery** from the **College of Physicians and Surgeons Pakistan (CPSP)**. It is awarded after years of supervised training in recognised hospital units and demanding examinations.
- **Where did you train?** Training in a busy plastic and reconstructive surgery unit exposes surgeons to a wide range of operations and complications.
- **Do you regularly perform this procedure?** A surgeon may be well qualified but focus on different areas.
- **Are you a member of a professional body?** For example, the Pakistan Association of Plastic Surgeons.

For non-surgical treatments such as Botox, fillers or skin treatments, a suitably trained dermatologist may also be a good choice. For operations, look for a qualified plastic surgeon.

## Step 3: Ask about the facility and who does what

A skilled surgeon still needs a safe place to operate.

- **Where will the surgery take place?** Is it a properly registered hospital or day-care facility? In Punjab, healthcare establishments are regulated by the Punjab Healthcare Commission.
- **Who gives the anaesthesia?** For general anaesthesia or deep sedation, a qualified anaesthetist should be present.
- **What happens if something goes wrong?** Ask whether the facility can manage an emergency and whether an overnight stay is possible if needed.
- **Who does each step?** This matters particularly for hair transplants, where trained technicians often assist. Ask who designs the hairline, who makes the openings for the grafts and whether the surgeon is present throughout.

## Step 4: Use your consultation well

A proper consultation with the surgeon, not only a coordinator, is essential. Take notes, bring a family member if you like, and ask:

- Am I a good candidate for this procedure? What will it not be able to do for me?
- Which technique do you recommend for me, and why? What are the alternatives, including non-surgical options or doing nothing?
- Will you personally perform my surgery?
- What are the main risks in my case, and how would a complication be handled?
- How long is the recovery, and how much time off work should I plan?
- How do follow-up visits work, and who do I contact after surgery?
- Can I see before-and-after photos of your own patients who had this procedure?
- What exactly does the quote include? For example tests, anaesthesia, hospital stay, garments, medicines and follow-up.
- If a revision is needed, how is it handled?

A good surgeon welcomes these questions. You should leave the consultation understanding both the benefits and the downsides.

## Red flags to watch for

| Reassuring signs | Warning signs |
|---|---|
| PMDC number shared without hesitation | Vague answers about registration or training |
| Specialist training in plastic surgery | Titles but no clear specialist qualification |
| In-depth consultation with the surgeon | Booked by a salesperson; surgeon first seen on the day |
| Risks explained clearly | Risks brushed aside as "nothing to worry about" |
| Realistic, honest expectations | Promises of perfect, certain or "100%" results |
| Clear, itemised quote | Very cheap deals and pressure to pay a deposit today |
| Registered hospital or day-care facility | Surgery in a salon or unregistered premises |
| Clear aftercare plan | Hard to reach once you have paid |
| Before-and-after photos of the surgeon's own patients | Stock images or photos that appear on several other clinics' pages |

Take extra care with "limited-time" packages, offers to combine several procedures at a discount, or anyone who tells you not to bother with a consultation. Surgery is not something to rush into because a deal ends on Friday.

## A word about price

Cost is a fair thing to compare. But when one quote is far lower than others, ask why. Savings sometimes come from fewer grafts than promised, an unregistered facility, no anaesthetist, or little aftercare.

Compare what each quote actually includes, not just the final figure. Dr. Junaid's [cost guide](/cost-guide/) explains the factors that affect the price of surgery, and a personalised quote follows your consultation.

## Social media is not a qualification

A large following, polished videos or celebrity clients do not tell you how safe or skilled a surgeon is. Reviews can help, but look for detailed, consistent ones over time. Remember that some online content is sponsored. Always come back to the basics: registration, specialist training, a safe facility and an honest consultation.

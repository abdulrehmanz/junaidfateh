PRP suits early thinning, where hair follicles are still alive but producing weaker hair: it may reduce shedding and improve thickness, but it cannot grow hair where follicles have been lost. A hair transplant is the option for areas that are bald or very thin, because it moves permanent follicles from the back of the head to where they are needed. Many people do best with both, using a transplant for the bald areas and PRP or medication to protect the rest.

Below, Dr. Junaid Fateh explains how each treatment works, compares them side by side and describes the situations where one, the other or both make sense.

## What PRP does

PRP stands for platelet-rich plasma. A small amount of blood is taken from your arm and spun in a centrifuge to concentrate the platelets. That plasma is then injected into the thinning parts of your scalp. Platelets release growth factors that are thought to support weakened follicles and help them produce thicker hair for longer.

A session takes around 30 to 60 minutes and needs no more than topical numbing, if any. Most people go back to normal activities the same day. Treatment is given as a course, usually several sessions about a month apart, followed by top-ups every few months if you respond.

The research on PRP is encouraging but mixed. Some people notice less hair fall and fuller hair within a few months, while others see little change. It works best when follicles are still present, and its effect tends to fade without maintenance. Read more on the [PRP hair therapy](/procedures/prp-hair-therapy/) page.

## What a hair transplant does

A hair transplant moves follicles from the back and sides of your scalp, where hair is usually resistant to pattern baldness, into the thinning or bald areas. The grafts are collected either one at a time ([FUE](/procedures/fue-hair-transplant/)) or from a thin strip ([FUT](/procedures/fut-hair-transplant/)).

It is done under local anaesthetic, usually takes most of a day, and most people return to desk work within a few days to two weeks depending on the method. New hair usually starts to appear around three to four months, and the full result takes twelve to eighteen months. Because transplanted hair keeps its resistance, the result is generally long-lasting.

A transplant does not create new hair. It redistributes the hair you have, and your donor supply is limited. It also does not stop the rest of your hair from thinning.

## Which one fits your situation?

**You notice more hair in the shower and a slightly wider parting, but no bald patches.** PRP and medical treatment are often the first step, once the cause has been checked.

**Your hairline has receded and the temples are bare.** PRP cannot bring back a hairline that has gone. A transplant is usually needed for a visible change, often with medication to protect the hair behind it.

**Your crown is thinning, but hair is still present.** Medical treatment or PRP may be tried first. If the area becomes bald or treatment does not help enough, a transplant can be considered.

**You are young and your hair loss is moving fast.** Stabilising your hair usually comes before surgery. The guide on the [best age for a hair transplant](/blog/best-age-for-hair-transplant/) explains why.

**You have already had a transplant.** PRP may help support your existing hair around the grafts.

**You are a woman with diffuse thinning.** The cause is investigated first, and medical treatment or PRP is usually preferred. A transplant suits only selected women with a stable, defined area of loss.

**You had sudden heavy shedding after an illness, stress or childbirth.** This is often temporary and settles once the cause passes. Neither treatment may be needed, but it is worth having the cause checked.

## Using both together

PRP and a transplant do different jobs, so they can work well together. PRP may be used before surgery to strengthen thinning hair near the area being treated, or afterwards to support the healing scalp and your existing hair. The evidence for combining them is still developing, so Dr. Junaid recommends it only where he thinks it is likely to help.

## When each is not suitable

PRP may be delayed or avoided if you have certain blood or platelet disorders, take blood-thinning medicines, have an active scalp infection or are pregnant. A transplant may not be advised if your donor area is too thin, your hair loss is very early and unpredictable, your health is not well controlled, or your expectations are beyond what your donor hair can achieve.

Most people return to office work and social life around one to two weeks after rhinoplasty, once the splint is off and the bruising has faded. The nose still looks and feels slightly swollen for several months, and the final shape usually settles at around twelve months, sometimes longer if your skin is thick.

Rhinoplasty recovery happens in stages, and knowing what is normal at each one makes the wait much calmer. The timings below are typical ranges. Dr. Junaid Fateh's team gives you written aftercare for your own operation, and those instructions always come first.

## Before surgery: get ready to rest

- **Arrange help** for the first two or three days, and someone to take you home.
- **Stock up** on soft foods, water, lip balm and saline spray if advised.
- **Prepare pillows** so you can sleep with your head raised.
- **Stop smoking** for as long as advised before and after surgery, since it slows healing.
- **Check your medicines.** Some medicines and supplements increase bleeding. Tell Dr. Junaid about everything you take, and do not stop prescribed medicines without his advice.

## The day of surgery

[Rhinoplasty](/procedures/rhinoplasty/) is usually a day case or involves one night in hospital. You wake up with a splint on the outside of your nose and sometimes soft supports inside. Your nose will feel blocked, so you breathe through your mouth, which makes your throat dry.

A small gauze pad under the nose catches light oozing, which is normal for the first day or two. Discomfort is usually described as pressure and stuffiness rather than sharp pain, and it is managed with the medicines you are given.

## Week 1: splint, swelling and bruising

Swelling and bruising around the eyes usually peak on days two to three and then begin to fade.

- Sleep with your head raised on two or three pillows.
- Use cool compresses around the eyes if advised, without pressing on the splint.
- Do not blow your nose. If you need to sneeze, sneeze through your mouth.
- Avoid bending forward, lifting and straining.
- Keep the splint dry, so wash your face and hair carefully, or ask someone to help.

## Days 7 to 10: the splint comes off

The splint and any stitches are usually removed at your first review. Most people find this moment exciting, but your nose will still be swollen, and the tip may look puffy or slightly turned up. This is expected. It is far too early to judge the result.

Most remaining bruising can be covered with light make-up at this stage, and many people feel ready for office work and errands.

## Weeks 2 to 6: back to normal life, with care

You will look presentable in public, though the nose still feels firm, numb at the tip and a little blocked. Breathing usually improves as the internal swelling settles.

**Glasses:** avoid resting glasses on the bridge of the nose for around six weeks, or as long as advised. Options include taping them to the forehead or resting them on the cheeks. Contact lenses are usually fine once the eye swelling has gone down.

**Exercise:** walking from the first days. Light cardio often restarts around three weeks. Heavier gym work usually waits until about six weeks.

**Contact sports:** cricket, football, wrestling and anything else where a ball, elbow or fall could hit your face need to wait longer, usually a few months.

**Sun:** protect your nose from sunburn, especially in summer, and keep any small scar at the base of the nose out of strong sun.

## Months 3 to 6: the shape refines

Much of the swelling has now gone, and your profile looks clearer. Swelling tends to be worse in the morning and settles during the day. The tip and the skin over it are usually the last to settle.

## Months 6 to 12: the final result

The final shape appears gradually, often at around twelve months, and more slowly if you have thicker skin. Dr. Junaid reviews your progress along the way. If any further change is ever considered, it is usually discussed only once healing is complete, often at a year or more.

## Recovery at a glance

| Time after surgery | What is usually happening | What you can usually do |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1-3 | Blocked nose, swelling and bruising peak | Rest with head raised, walk around the house |
| Days 4-7 | Bruising fading | Light activity at home; no nose blowing |
| Days 7-10 | Splint and stitches removed | Office work and errands for many people |
| Weeks 2-6 | Firm, slightly swollen nose | Light cardio from around 3 weeks; no glasses on the bridge |
| Months 3-6 | Most swelling gone | Most normal activities, including the gym as advised |
| Months 6-12 | Final shape emerging | Review with Dr. Junaid |

## Warning signs: contact the team

Like any surgery, rhinoplasty carries risks, including bleeding, infection, breathing difficulty, asymmetry and a result that does not fully meet expectations. Dr. Junaid goes through these with you at consultation. Message the team on WhatsApp, or seek urgent care, if you have:

- Bleeding that does not slow after ten to fifteen minutes of sitting upright, leaning forward and resting quietly
- Fever, or spreading redness and swelling
- Pain that keeps getting worse instead of better
- Any knock or injury to the nose during recovery

## Planning around weddings and travel

Because the nose keeps refining for months, avoid booking rhinoplasty in the weeks before a wedding, Eid or important photographs. If you want to look your best for a particular date, speak to Dr. Junaid well in advance.

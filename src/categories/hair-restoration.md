Losing hair can change how you feel about photographs, weddings and even everyday meetings. ## Why hair loss happens

The most common cause in men is androgenetic alopecia, better known as male pattern baldness. It runs in families. Follicles on the front and top of the head are sensitive to DHT, a hormone made from testosterone, and gradually shrink until they produce only fine, short hairs or none at all. Hair at the back and sides is usually resistant, and that is what makes a transplant possible.

Women can have a similar inherited pattern, usually seen as a widening parting rather than a receding hairline. Other causes include heavy shedding a few months after a high fever such as dengue or typhoid, stress, childbirth, crash dieting, low iron, thyroid problems, tight hairstyles and the patchy loss of alopecia areata. Many of these are temporary or treatable without surgery, which is why a proper diagnosis comes before any talk of grafts.

## The Norwood scale in plain words

Doctors describe male hair loss using the Norwood scale, which runs from stage 1 to stage 7:

- **Stages 1-2:** little or no loss, perhaps a slight rise at the temples.
- **Stage 3:** the temples recede into a clear M shape, sometimes with early thinning on the crown.
- **Stages 4-5:** the front and crown thin further, with a band of hair still separating them.
- **Stages 6-7:** that band has gone, leaving hair mainly in a horseshoe around the sides and back.

Your stage helps Dr. Junaid judge how many grafts you may need and whether your donor area can cover it. The graft estimator on this page gives a rough starting figure, and the real number is set at consultation.

## FUE or FUT?

Both methods move the same thing: natural groups of one to four hairs called follicular units. FUE takes them out one at a time, leaving tiny dot marks that are usually hard to see once your hair grows back, and suits people who keep their hair short. FUT removes a thin strip of scalp that is divided into grafts, leaving a fine line hidden under hair of moderate length, and can provide a large number of grafts in a single session. Dr. Junaid recommends one, or occasionally both, based on your scalp, the extent of your loss and your preferred hairstyle. FUE itself can be carried out in different ways, including [Sapphire FUE, DHI and robotic FUE](/procedures/fue-hair-transplant/), and he will explain which suits your hair.

## Who is a good candidate?

You are likely to be suitable if your hair loss pattern has settled enough to plan around, you have good density at the back and sides, and you are in reasonable health, with conditions such as diabetes or high blood pressure under control. Realistic expectations matter just as much. A transplant redistributes your own hair rather than creating new follicles, so very advanced loss can be improved but not always fully reversed. Younger men with early thinning are often better served by medication or PRP first. If you smoke, you will be asked to stop around the time of surgery, as smoking can slow healing.

## Designing a natural hairline

The hairline is what people notice first, so Dr. Junaid draws it by hand and refines it with you in the mirror. He sets it at a height that will still suit you at fifty, not only at thirty, softens the front edge with fine single hairs and keeps the temples in proportion with your face. Each graft is angled forward along your natural hair direction, so the result blends in rather than standing up like a brush.

## What the day looks like

Most hair transplants are day cases. You arrive in the morning, confirm the hairline and have photographs taken. Local anaesthetic numbs the donor and recipient areas, after which most people feel pressure rather than pain. Grafts are taken, sorted and placed over several hours, with breaks for meals and prayer. You go home the same evening with written instructions, your medicines and a WhatsApp contact for the team.

## Aftercare and the months ahead

For the first few nights you sleep with your head raised and follow the washing routine the team shows you, which loosens the crusts over the next one to two weeks. Heavy exercise, swimming, strong sun and smoking are avoided for as long as advised. The transplanted hairs usually shed between two and six weeks, then new growth starts at around three to four months. Most people see a clear change by six to nine months, with the final result at 12-18 months.

Results vary between people. Like any surgery, a hair transplant carries risks, such as swelling, infection, numbness and uneven growth, and Dr. Junaid goes through these with you at consultation before following your progress as the new hair comes in.

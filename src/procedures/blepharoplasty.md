## About blepharoplasty

The skin of the eyelids is the thinnest on the body, so it is often one of the first places to show age. Upper lids can gather into heavy folds that rest on the lashes, while fat around the eye can push forward and form bags underneath. The result is a tired or even sad expression that does not match how you feel.

Blepharoplasty, or eyelid surgery, removes or repositions the excess skin, muscle and fat behind these changes. It can be done on the upper lids, the lower lids or both. For some people the benefit is mainly cosmetic. For others, heavy upper lids have started to narrow their view, and lifting them makes everyday tasks such as driving or reading easier.

Dr. Junaid Fateh performs eyelid surgery in Lahore as part of his facial plastic surgery work. Because the eyes are so central to expression, small and precise changes matter more here than almost anywhere else on the face.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches eyelid surgery

At your consultation, Dr. Junaid looks closely at the whole upper face, not only the eyelids. He checks whether the heaviness comes from the eyelid skin, from a lowered brow, or from weakness of the muscle that opens the eye. That last condition, called ptosis, needs a different repair. He also asks about dry eyes, vision problems, thyroid conditions and any medicines that thin the blood.

His aim is fresh, open eyes that still look like yours. Taking away too much skin or fat can leave the eyes looking hollow or make them hard to close, so he plans conservatively and, where it suits you, repositions fat rather than simply removing it. Your natural crease, eye shape and family features are respected.

Measurements and photographs help him plan how much skin to remove from each side, since most people have some natural asymmetry. Every operation carries some risk, and eyelid surgery is no exception. Dr. Junaid explains the possible complications in plain language so you can make a fully informed decision.

## Techniques and options

Depending on your eyes, Dr. Junaid may suggest:

- **Upper blepharoplasty:** Removes excess upper lid skin, and sometimes a little fat, through an incision hidden in the natural crease.
- **Lower blepharoplasty (skin approach):** An incision just below the lower lashes allows loose skin to be removed and fat to be adjusted for smoother under-eyes.
- **Transconjunctival blepharoplasty:** Under-eye fat is treated from inside the lower eyelid, leaving no visible scar on the skin. It suits people with puffiness but good skin tone.
- **Fat repositioning:** Instead of being removed, under-eye fat is moved into the hollow at the top of the cheek to soften the tear trough.
- **Combined treatment:** Eyelid surgery is often paired with a brow lift, a facelift or non-surgical treatments when more than one area needs attention.
- **Asian (double eyelid) blepharoplasty:** Creates or refines an upper eyelid crease while respecting the natural shape of the eye, using a small incision or a stitch technique depending on the lid.

## What to expect on the day

Most eyelid surgery is a day case. The area is numbed with local anaesthetic and you are given sedation to help you relax. Surgery itself typically lasts between one and two hours. A family member should take you home the same day, as you should not drive, and you leave with cool compresses and clear aftercare instructions.

## About Breast Reconstruction

Breast reconstruction rebuilds the shape of a breast after a mastectomy or lumpectomy, or when a breast has not developed normally from birth. It can be carried out at the same time as cancer surgery or months, even years, afterwards.

For many women, reconstruction is part of feeling whole again after a very difficult time. For others it is not a priority, and that is just as valid. What matters is that you understand your options and make the choice that is right for you and your family.

Dr. Junaid Fateh completed his plastic surgery training at the Jinnah Burn & Reconstructive Surgery Centre in Lahore, and reconstructive surgery is a core part of his work. He aims to give you a breast that looks balanced in clothes and feels like part of your body, while being honest that a reconstructed breast will not be identical to the original.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches breast reconstruction

Your first consultation is unhurried. Dr. Junaid asks about your diagnosis and treatment plan, especially whether radiotherapy has been given or is planned, because it affects which method is safest and when it should be done. He examines your chest and, if a tissue flap might be used, your tummy or back. Your general health, weight and smoking history also shape the plan.

Reconstruction is usually planned in stages: building the breast mound first, then refining its shape, matching the other side and finally recreating the nipple. Each stage is explained before you agree to it, and you can pause between stages if you need to.

Like any major surgery, reconstruction carries risks. These include infection, wound healing problems, implant complications such as hardening or the need for replacement, partial or, rarely, complete loss of a tissue flap, and weakness or a bulge at the donor site. Dr. Junaid goes through the risks of your chosen method in detail.

## Techniques and options

Depending on your treatment and your body, options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Tissue expander then implant:** a temporary expander gently stretches the skin over several weeks or months before a permanent implant is placed.
- **Direct-to-implant reconstruction:** for selected women at the time of mastectomy, a permanent implant is placed in a single stage.
- **Latissimus dorsi flap:** skin and muscle from the back are moved round to the chest, often together with an implant.
- **Abdominal tissue flaps (TRAM or DIEP):** skin and fat from the lower tummy are used to build a soft, natural breast. A DIEP flap spares the tummy muscle and requires microsurgery.
- **Fat grafting:** your own fat is used to fill dents after a lumpectomy or to refine an earlier reconstruction.
- **Symmetry surgery and nipple reconstruction:** the other breast can be lifted or reduced to match, and a nipple and areola recreated with local tissue and medical tattooing.

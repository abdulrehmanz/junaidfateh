## About Cleft Lip & Palate Surgery

A cleft is a gap in the upper lip, the roof of the mouth (the palate), or both, that forms while a baby is developing before birth. It is one of the more common differences a baby can be born with, and it is nobody's fault. With well-timed surgery and good follow-up, children with a cleft can grow up eating, talking and smiling with confidence.

A cleft lip mainly affects how the lip and nose look, and it can make feeding a little harder. A cleft palate has a bigger effect on daily life: milk can come out through the nose, ear infections are more common, and speech may sound nasal if the palate does not work properly. That is why cleft care is a journey of several stages rather than a single operation.

Dr. Junaid Fateh is a plastic and reconstructive surgeon with FCPS in Plastic Surgery. He repairs cleft lip and palate in babies and also treats older children and adults who have concerns left over from earlier repairs.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches cleft surgery

The first visit often happens soon after birth, when parents have many questions. Dr. Junaid examines your baby, explains the type of cleft in simple terms, and gives practical feeding advice. He then sets out a timeline for each stage, so the whole family knows what lies ahead. Families travelling from other cities can plan their visits around this timeline.

Each operation is planned around your child's age, weight and health. In lip repair, he rejoins the ring of lip muscle as well as the skin, lines up the edge of the red lip carefully, and supports the nostril so the result looks balanced and natural. In palate repair, he closes the gap and repositions the soft palate muscles to help with speech.

Safety comes first with babies. Surgery is booked only when your child is well, and you receive clear instructions about feeding, fasting and medicines beforehand. Dr. Junaid is honest that results vary between children and that some will need further procedures as they grow. Like any surgery, cleft repair has risks, and he goes through them with you at consultation.

He also reminds families that surgery is one part of cleft care. Speech therapy, hearing checks and dental or orthodontic treatment matter just as much, and he will guide you on when each is needed.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend, depending on age and need, include:

- **Primary cleft lip repair:** Closing the lip and rebuilding the muscle, usually at around three to six months.
- **Primary nasal correction:** Repositioning the nostril at the time of lip repair to improve symmetry.
- **Cleft palate repair:** Closing the roof of the mouth and repositioning the soft palate muscles, usually at around nine to twelve months.
- **Speech surgery:** Procedures such as a palate re-repair or a pharyngeal flap when speech stays nasal despite therapy.
- **Alveolar bone grafting:** Filling the gap in the gum line with bone, often taken from the hip, to support the adult teeth.
- **Lip and scar revision:** Refining the scar, lip line or fullness as the face grows.
- **Cleft rhinoplasty:** Reshaping the nose in the teenage years or adulthood, once facial growth has largely finished.

## What to expect on the day

Your child will need to stop feeding for a set time before the anaesthetic, and the team will tell you exactly when. The operation usually takes one to three hours. Afterwards your child is monitored closely, and most go home within one to three days with written instructions for feeding and wound care.

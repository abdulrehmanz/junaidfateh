## About Congenital Deformities

Some children are born with a part of the body that has formed differently. It might be an extra finger, two fingers joined together, an ear that is small or unusually shaped, or a birthmark. Doctors call these congenital differences or deformities. They are nobody's fault, and many can be improved with well-timed surgery.

Dr. Junaid Fateh is a plastic and reconstructive surgeon in Lahore who treats congenital conditions of the hand, ear, face and skin. His aim is always twofold: to help the affected part work as well as it can, and to help it look as natural as possible so your child can grow up feeling comfortable.

Not every difference needs an operation, and not every operation should happen straight away. Some conditions are best treated in the first year or two of life, while others are safer to correct when a child is older. Adults who were never treated as children can also be assessed.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches congenital deformity correction

The first visit is about understanding your child, not rushing towards theatre. Dr. Junaid examines the area, watches how it moves and is used in play, and may ask for an X-ray or other tests. He explains what can realistically be achieved, what cannot, and whether waiting would be the wiser choice.

Timing is planned around growth. Hands, ears and faces keep changing through childhood, so some corrections are done in stages with check-ups in between. You will know the likely sequence from the beginning, which helps the family plan school, travel and time off.

Safety comes first for young patients. Surgery under general anaesthesia is carried out in a hospital setting with an anaesthetist experienced in caring for children. Where a condition is better managed jointly with another specialist, such as a paediatrician, orthopaedic surgeon or therapist, Dr. Junaid coordinates that care or refers you on. As with any operation, there are risks such as bleeding, infection, scarring and occasionally the need for further surgery, and Dr. Junaid goes through these with you at consultation.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Extra finger or thumb (polydactyly):** removing the extra digit and, where needed, rebalancing the joint, tendons and ligaments so the remaining finger or thumb lines up and works well.
- **Joined fingers (syndactyly):** separating the fingers with carefully designed skin flaps, often with a small skin graft, so each finger can move freely.
- **Trigger thumb in children:** releasing the tight tunnel that stops the thumb from straightening fully.
- **Ear differences:** reshaping prominent or folded ears, and staged reconstruction for a small or underdeveloped ear (microtia) once the child is old enough.
- **Birthmarks and skin lesions:** surgical removal of selected moles, vascular lesions and skin tags, with scars placed as discreetly as possible. Some birthmarks are better treated without surgery, and Dr. Junaid will tell you if so.
- **Other limb and facial differences:** constriction bands, tight skin and some facial asymmetries can often be improved with local flaps, grafts or fat transfer.
- **Chest wall differences:** Underdeveloped chest muscle or breast (as in Poland syndrome) and some chest shape differences can be improved with fat transfer, implants or tissue flaps once growth is complete.

## What to expect on the day

Your child will need to stop eating and drinking for a few hours beforehand, and the team will give you exact timings. A parent can usually stay until your child is asleep and is there again in the recovery area. Most hand and ear procedures finish with a dressing or splint, and the hospital stay is usually one to three days depending on the operation.

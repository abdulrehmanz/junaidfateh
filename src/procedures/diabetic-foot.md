## About Diabetic Foot Management

Diabetes can quietly damage the nerves and blood vessels in the feet. When feeling is reduced, a small blister, crack or cut from a tight shoe can go unnoticed for days. With weaker circulation and high blood sugar, that small wound heals slowly and can become infected. Diabetes is common in Pakistan, and foot problems are among its most serious complications.

Dr. Junaid Fateh, a plastic and reconstructive surgeon in Lahore and Layyah, treats foot ulcers, infections and wounds in people with diabetes. His focus is on healing the wound, controlling infection and keeping as much of the foot as possible working, so you can stay on your feet and independent.

**Seek urgent care for spreading infection.** If your foot becomes red, hot or swollen, the redness is spreading, there is pus or a bad smell, the skin is turning dark, or you have a fever or unusually high sugars, go to the nearest emergency department the same day. Fast treatment can make the difference between saving and losing part of the foot.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches diabetic foot care

Good diabetic foot care is teamwork. Dr. Junaid works alongside your physician or diabetologist, because steady blood sugar is one of the most important factors in healing. Where circulation is poor he involves a vascular specialist, since blood flow may need to be improved before a wound can close.

At consultation he examines both feet, checks feeling and pulses, measures the wound and looks for signs of deeper infection. An X-ray or other scan may be needed to check the bone. You leave with a clear plan, including how the wound will be dressed and how to keep weight off it.

He is honest about what is possible. Many wounds heal with the right mix of cleaning, dressings and off-loading. Sometimes removing infected tissue, or occasionally a toe, is the safest way to protect the rest of the foot and leg. Foot surgery in diabetes carries risks such as infection, slow healing and the need for further procedures, and Dr. Junaid talks you through these before any treatment.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Debridement:** removing dead and infected tissue to give the wound a clean, healthy base. This may need repeating.
- **Drainage of infection:** opening and draining a collection of pus, alongside antibiotics guided by swab or tissue results.
- **Off-loading:** special dressings, footwear, insoles or a cast to take pressure off the ulcer while it heals.
- **Negative pressure wound therapy:** a suction dressing that helps clean, deep wounds fill in with healthy tissue.
- **Skin grafting:** covering a clean wound with a thin layer of your own skin.
- **Local flap surgery:** moving nearby tissue with its own blood supply to cover exposed bone or tendon.
- **Foot-care education:** daily checks, nail care and footwear advice to help stop new ulcers forming.
- **Limb salvage:** Combining infection control, off-loading, grafts and flaps with your diabetes and vascular care to save as much of the foot as possible and avoid amputation where it can be avoided.

## What to expect on the day

Bring your diabetes medicines, recent sugar readings and any reports. Smaller procedures are often done under local or regional anaesthetic. If a general anaesthetic is planned, the team will tell you how to adjust your diabetes medicines while fasting. Afterwards you will be shown how to care for the dressing and how to rest the foot at home.

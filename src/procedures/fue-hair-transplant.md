## About FUE hair transplant

FUE stands for follicular unit extraction. Instead of removing a strip of skin, hair is taken one follicular unit at a time, usually from the back and sides of the head, and each graft is placed into the thinning area. A follicular unit is the natural cluster of one to four hairs that grows together from the scalp, so the transplanted hair grows in the same small groups as the rest of your hair.

The back and sides of the scalp are usually resistant to the hormone-driven thinning behind male pattern baldness. Hair moved from there tends to keep that resistance in its new position, which is why a well-planned FUE transplant is a long-term treatment. The hair you have not had transplanted can still thin with time, so planning for the future is part of every consultation.

Because there is no strip, FUE does not leave a linear scar. It does leave many tiny round marks in the donor area. Once your hair grows back these are usually very hard to see, even with a fairly short haircut.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches FUE hair transplant

Dr. Junaid Fateh plans a hair transplant with the same care as any other operation. At your consultation he examines your scalp, the pattern and stage of your hair loss, and the density and quality of your donor hair. He also asks about your health, medicines and family history. If the reason for your hair fall is unclear, he may suggest blood tests before deciding on surgery.

He then talks honestly about what is realistic. Donor hair is limited, so the aim is to use it where it makes the most visible difference while keeping some in reserve in case you need more work later. Your hairline is drawn on before surgery and adjusted with you in the mirror until it looks right. He designs it to suit your age and face, with a soft, slightly irregular edge and fine single hairs at the front, because a hairline that is too low or too straight is what usually gives a transplant away.

Safety runs through every step. The procedure is carried out under sterile conditions, the graft count stays within what your donor area can safely give, and Dr. Junaid goes through the possible risks with you at consultation, including swelling, temporary numbness, infection, shedding of existing hair and uneven growth.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Hairline restoration:** rebuilding a receding front and temples with carefully angled single- and two-hair grafts.
- **Crown treatment:** filling the thinning patch on the back of the top of the head, where hair grows in a swirl that has to be copied closely.
- **Channel and placement method:** the way recipient sites are opened and grafts are placed is chosen to suit your hair type and the area being treated.
- **Staged sessions:** for larger areas, the work may be split into two sessions several months apart to protect graft quality and the donor area.
- **Beard hair as extra donor:** in selected cases with limited scalp donor hair, hair from under the chin may add to the grafts available.
- **Combined treatment:** medication or PRP hair therapy may be advised to help protect the hair you already have.
- **Sapphire FUE:** Recipient channels are opened with fine sapphire blades, which allows small, precise openings and closely packed grafts.
- **DHI (direct hair implantation):** Grafts are loaded into a pen-like implanter and placed directly, which gives fine control over angle and depth, especially along the hairline.
- **Body hair transplant:** For people with very limited scalp donor hair, hair from the beard area or chest may be used to add density in selected cases.
- **Large single sessions:** When the donor area allows, a higher number of grafts can be moved in one longer session, so long as graft quality is not compromised.
- **Robotic FUE:** A robotic system assists with mapping and extracting follicles in a consistent pattern. Dr. Junaid still designs the hairline and plans graft placement himself, and will advise whether robotic or manual extraction suits your hair type.

## What to expect on the day

You arrive in the morning, the hairline is confirmed and the donor area is trimmed short. Local anaesthetic numbs the scalp, after which most people feel only pressure. Extraction and placement fill most of the day, with pauses for meals and namaz along the way. You leave the same day with written aftercare instructions and a WhatsApp contact for the team.

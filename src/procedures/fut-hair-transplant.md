## About FUT hair transplant

FUT, or follicular unit transplantation, is often called the strip method. A narrow strip of hair-bearing skin is removed from the back of the scalp and the wound is stitched closed. The strip is then divided under magnification into individual follicular units, and these grafts are placed into the thinning areas in much the same way as in FUE.

The main strength of FUT is efficiency. Because the grafts come from one concentrated area, a large number can often be obtained in a single session while the rest of the donor zone is left alone. That makes it a practical choice if your hair loss is more advanced, or if you would like to keep the wider donor area in reserve for FUE in the future.

The trade-off is a linear scar across the back of the head. In most people it heals as a fine line that is covered by hair of moderate length. Scars do vary from person to person, though, and one may show if you later decide to keep your hair very short.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches FUT hair transplant

As a plastic surgeon, Dr. Junaid Fateh pays close attention to how the donor wound is planned and closed, because the quality of the scar matters as much as the grafts. At consultation he checks the looseness of your scalp, the density at the back of your head and how you like to wear your hair. If your scalp is tight or you prefer very short styles, he will tell you plainly that FUE may suit you better.

He takes only as wide a strip as your scalp can close comfortably, which reduces tension on the wound and helps the scar heal flat. Your new hairline is designed with you beforehand, with natural irregularity at the front and grafts angled to follow your own hair direction. He also plans ahead, so that the grafts used now still look right if your hair loss progresses.

Like any surgery, FUT carries risks. Dr. Junaid goes through them with you at consultation, including a wider or stretched scar, numbness or tightness at the back of the head that usually settles, infection, swelling and uneven growth.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Single-strip harvest:** one carefully measured strip from the safe donor zone, which is usually the densest part of the scalp.
- **Graft dissection under magnification:** the strip is divided into one-, two- and three-hair units, with fine singles reserved for the hairline.
- **Trichophytic closure:** a closure technique that lets hair grow through the edge of the scar, which can help it blend in.
- **Repeat FUT through an old scar:** if you have had FUT before, the previous scar can often be included in the new strip so you are left with one line.
- **FUT combined with FUE:** where a very large number of grafts is needed, both methods may be used to make the most of the donor area.

## What to expect on the day

Once the hairline is agreed, the back of your scalp is numbed and the strip is removed and closed. You can rest while the team prepares the grafts. Placement into the thinning area takes most of the day. A light sedative may be offered to help you relax, and you go home the same day with a dressing and written aftercare instructions.

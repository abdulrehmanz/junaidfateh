## About Gynecomastia

Gynecomastia is the medical name for enlarged breast tissue in men. It may affect one side or both, and it ranges from a small, puffy swelling behind the nipple to a full, rounded chest that shows through a shirt. It is common and harmless in itself, yet many men live with it quietly for years because it feels awkward to talk about.

Dr. Junaid Fateh is a plastic, cosmetic and reconstructive surgeon who sees patients in Lahore and Layyah, and male chest surgery is one of his signature specialties. Surgery removes the extra gland and fat, reshapes the chest and, where needed, tightens loose skin, so your chest looks flatter, firmer and in proportion with the rest of your body.

## What causes gynecomastia?

Most cases come down to a change in the balance between oestrogen and testosterone. Common triggers include the hormonal shifts of puberty, weight gain, anabolic steroids and some bodybuilding supplements, and certain prescription medicines. Less often, liver, kidney or thyroid problems or other hormone conditions play a part, and in many men no single cause is found.

Some men have pseudogynecomastia, where the fullness is mostly fat rather than gland. Telling the two apart matters, because it decides which technique will work.

## Grades of gynecomastia

Surgeons use slightly different grading systems, but in plain terms:

- **Grade 1:** a small, firm button of tissue under the areola, often noticed as puffy nipples under a T-shirt.
- **Grade 2:** wider fullness across the chest with little or no loose skin.
- **Grade 3:** moderate enlargement with some extra skin, so the chest starts to sag.
- **Grade 4:** a large, breast-like chest with clear skin excess, where skin removal is often needed.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches gynecomastia

At your consultation, Dr. Junaid listens to what bothers you, asks about medicines, supplements and gym history, and examines your chest to feel how much is firm gland and how much is soft fat. He also checks skin tone, nipple position and any difference between the two sides. If a medical cause seems possible, he may suggest blood tests or a check-up first.

His aim is a natural, athletic look rather than an over-hollowed chest. A thin layer of tissue is usually left under the nipple to prevent a sunken, dish-shaped area, and the edges are blended into the armpit and upper tummy so there is no visible step. Like any surgery, gynecomastia correction carries risks, including bleeding or a collection of blood, fluid build-up, an uneven contour, changes in nipple sensation and scarring. Dr. Junaid goes through these with you at consultation.

## Techniques and options

Liposuction removes soft fat well but struggles with dense gland, which usually has to be cut out directly. That is why many men need a combination. Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Liposuction alone:** for a mainly fatty chest with good skin tone, using a fine tube through tiny incisions.
- **Gland excision:** removal of the firm disc of gland through a small cut along the lower edge of the areola.
- **Combined liposuction and excision:** the most common approach for true gynecomastia, removing the gland and smoothing the surrounding fat in one operation.
- **Puffy nipple correction:** a focused removal of a small amount of gland for slim or athletic men whose concern is limited to the nipple area.
- **Skin reduction:** for higher grades or after major weight loss, where extra skin is removed and the nipple may be repositioned, leaving longer scars.

You arrive having fasted as instructed, and Dr. Junaid marks your chest while you stand. The operation usually takes one to two hours under general anaesthesia or local anaesthesia with sedation. Most men go home the same day wearing a compression vest, so arrange for someone to drive you.

## About Hand Surgery

Your hands do almost everything for you, from writing and eating to earning a living. When an injury or condition stops them working properly, it touches every part of your day. Hand surgery repairs the bones, tendons, nerves, blood vessels and skin of the hand and wrist so you can grip, pinch, feel and move again.

Dr. Junaid Fateh is a plastic and reconstructive surgeon with FCPS in Plastic Surgery who trained at Jinnah Burn & Reconstructive Surgery Centre in Lahore. Hand and reconstructive surgery is one of his signature areas. He treats fresh injuries, such as glass cuts, fodder-cutter (toka) machine accidents and crushed or broken fingers, as well as longer-standing problems like carpal tunnel syndrome, trigger finger, Dupuytren's contracture, lumps, and stiffness left behind by an old injury or burn.

If you have just hurt your hand, go to the nearest emergency department first. The team there can control bleeding, clean the wound, give a tetanus injection and X-ray the hand. Specialist repair of tendons and nerves can then follow, ideally without delay.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches hand surgery

Consultation starts with listening. Dr. Junaid asks how the problem began, which hand you write with, what work you do and what you most want to get back to. He then tests each tendon and nerve in turn, checks the feeling in your fingertips and reviews any X-rays or nerve studies. This careful examination tells him exactly which structures are involved.

Planning puts function first, although appearance still matters. Incisions follow the natural creases of the hand where possible so scars settle well. Tendons and nerves are repaired under magnification, and broken bones are held with wires, screws or small plates only when a splint alone will not keep them aligned.

Safety guides every decision. Dr. Junaid chooses the lightest anaesthetic that suits the operation, which for many procedures means a local or regional block so you can stay awake and go home the same day. He is also honest about what your hand is likely to achieve. Results vary between people, and a badly damaged hand may improve a great deal without becoming exactly as it was. Like any surgery, hand surgery carries risks, and Dr. Junaid goes through them with you at consultation.

Rehabilitation is treated as part of the operation. You leave with a splint where needed and a clear exercise plan, and his team follows your progress in the weeks that follow.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Tendon repair:** Rejoining a cut or torn tendon so the finger can bend or straighten again, followed by a protected exercise programme.
- **Nerve repair and grafting:** Bringing the ends of a cut nerve together, or bridging a gap with a nerve graft, to give feeling and muscle control the best chance to return.
- **Fracture fixation:** Realigning broken finger, hand or wrist bones and holding them in place when a splint is not enough.
- **Carpal tunnel release:** Freeing the median nerve at the wrist to ease numbness, tingling and night pain, usually under local anaesthetic.
- **Trigger finger release:** Opening the tight tunnel that the tendon catches on so the finger glides smoothly again.
- **Dupuytren's surgery:** Removing or dividing the thickened cords that pull the fingers into the palm.
- **Replantation and revascularisation:** Using microsurgery to reattach a cut-off finger or restore blood flow to an injured one, when the injury and timing allow.
- **Secondary reconstruction:** Tendon transfers, contracture release, skin grafts or flaps to improve an old injury that has healed badly.

## What to expect on the day

Most planned hand operations are day cases. You will be asked to remove rings beforehand, and the anaesthetic is given before the hand is cleaned and the surgery begins. Afterwards your hand is wrapped in a padded dressing or splint and kept raised in a sling. Before you leave, the team explains dressing care, which fingers to move and when to come back. Bring someone with you, as you will not be able to drive.

## About Limb Reconstruction

A serious injury to an arm or leg, from a road accident, a fall or farm and factory machinery, can damage skin, muscle, tendon, nerve and bone all at once. A long-standing bone infection, or the removal of a tumour, can leave a similar gap. Limb reconstruction is the surgery that repairs these areas so the limb is covered with healthy tissue, protected from infection and able to work again.

Dr. Junaid Fateh is an FCPS-qualified plastic and reconstructive surgeon practising in Lahore. In limb reconstruction his main role is rebuilding the soft tissues, the skin, fat and muscle that cover and nourish bone, tendons and any plates or rods. He works closely with orthopaedic surgeons, who manage the bone and its fixation.

**For a fresh injury, go to the nearest emergency department first.** Stopping bleeding, cleaning the wound, stabilising fractures and starting antibiotics all come before reconstruction. Dr. Junaid can then help plan how the wound will be covered.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches limb reconstruction

Every limb injury is different, so planning starts with a careful look at what has been lost and what is still healthy. Dr. Junaid examines the wound, the blood supply and the nerves, and reviews X-rays and scans with the orthopaedic team. Sometimes a scan of the blood vessels is needed before choosing a flap.

He works step by step, starting from the simplest method that will reliably do the job. That might be a skin graft, a flap of nearby tissue, or a free flap moved with microsurgery. The goal is a limb that is stable, well covered and useful, with the best appearance that can reasonably be achieved.

Honest discussion matters. In some very severe injuries, saving the limb may not give a better life than a carefully planned amputation and a good artificial limb, and Dr. Junaid will talk this through with you and your family. Limb reconstruction is major surgery, with risks that include infection, flap loss, slow bone healing, stiffness and the need for more operations, and he explains these at consultation.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Debridement and wound preparation:** removing damaged or infected tissue, sometimes over more than one operation, before the final reconstruction.
- **Skin grafting:** covering a clean wound bed with thin skin taken from the thigh or another area.
- **Local and pedicled flaps:** moving nearby muscle or skin, still attached to its own blood supply, to cover exposed bone or tendon.
- **Free flap reconstruction:** transferring tissue from another part of the body and reconnecting its vessels under the microscope when local tissue is not enough.
- **Bone reconstruction with the orthopaedic team:** bone grafts or a bone flap with its own blood supply to bridge a gap in the bone.
- **Nerve and tendon reconstruction:** repairing or grafting nerves, and tendon transfers to restore lost movement.
- **Negative pressure wound therapy:** a suction dressing used between stages to keep the wound clean and encourage healthy tissue.

## What to expect on the day

Reconstruction is usually done under general anaesthesia and can take several hours. After a flap, expect to stay in hospital for three to seven days with the limb raised and the flap checked regularly. Physiotherapy usually starts in hospital and continues once you are home.

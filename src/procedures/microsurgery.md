## About Microsurgery

Microsurgery is surgery carried out under high magnification, using an operating microscope with very fine instruments and stitches. It allows a surgeon to join blood vessels and nerves that are only a millimetre or two across. Rather than a single operation, it is a skill that makes several kinds of reconstruction possible.

Its best-known use is the free flap. A piece of tissue, such as skin, fat, muscle or bone, is lifted from one part of the body together with its own artery and vein. It is then moved to where it is needed and plugged into local blood vessels under the microscope. This lets a surgeon rebuild areas where no nearby tissue is suitable, for example after a serious leg injury, cancer surgery or a deep burn.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches microsurgery

Microsurgery is planned in detail. Dr. Junaid assesses the wound or injury, your general health and the blood vessels in the area, sometimes with a scan. He first asks whether a simpler method, such as a skin graft or a local flap, would do the job just as well, and recommends microsurgery only when it offers a clear advantage.

You will talk through where the tissue will come from, what scar it will leave there, and how the transferred tissue should look and work once healed. Smoking narrows blood vessels and raises the chance of a flap failing, so stopping well before surgery is strongly advised.

The first few days after a free flap matter most. The clinical team checks the flap's colour, warmth and blood flow at regular intervals, so that any problem with the vessel joins is spotted early. Microsurgery is major surgery with real risks, including bleeding, infection, clots, partial or complete flap loss and further operations, and Dr. Junaid goes through these with you at consultation.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Free flap reconstruction:** transferring skin, fat, muscle or bone with its blood supply to rebuild a defect elsewhere in the body.
- **Perforator flaps:** a refined type of free flap that takes skin and fat on small vessels while leaving the muscle underneath in place.
- **Nerve repair and nerve grafting:** joining a cut nerve end to end, or bridging a gap with a short length of nerve from elsewhere, to help feeling and movement return.
- **Replantation:** reattaching a finger, thumb or hand that has been cut off, when the injury and the amputated part make this possible.
- **Revascularisation:** restoring blood flow to a finger or limb that is still attached but whose vessels have been damaged.
- **Lymphatic surgery:** in selected patients with lymphoedema, joining tiny lymph channels to nearby veins to help fluid drain. Dr. Junaid will advise whether this is likely to help in your case.

Microsurgery is also used in breast reconstruction with your own tissue, which is described on its own page.

## What to expect on the day

Planned microsurgery takes place under general anaesthesia and usually lasts several hours. Expect to stay in hospital for three to seven days so the flap or repair can be watched closely. Keep warm, drink fluids when you are allowed to, and avoid smoking completely.

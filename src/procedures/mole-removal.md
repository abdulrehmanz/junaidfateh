## About Mole & Skin Tag Removal

Moles are small clusters of pigment cells and are extremely common. Most are harmless and stay the same for years. Skin tags are soft, small flaps of skin that tend to appear on the neck, underarms, eyelids and other places where skin rubs together. Neither usually needs treatment, but many people choose removal because of appearance, or because a lesion keeps catching on clothes, jewelry or a razor.

Sometimes a mole needs attention for medical reasons. Warning signs are often summarized as ABCDE: Asymmetry, an uneven Border, more than one Color, a Diameter larger than a pencil eraser, and Evolving, meaning a change in size, shape or color. A mole that itches, bleeds or looks different from your other moles also deserves a check rather than being ignored.

Dr. Junaid Fateh, a plastic, cosmetic and reconstructive surgeon in Lahore, removes moles and skin tags with attention to both safety and how the skin looks afterwards.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches mole removal

Every lesion is examined before anything is removed. Dr. Junaid looks at its size, color, border and history, and asks whether it has changed recently. This decides the safest method. A mole that looks entirely harmless can often be removed with a simple technique, while a mole with any worrying feature is cut out completely with a small margin of normal skin.

Suspicious moles are always sent to a laboratory for histopathology examination, where the tissue is studied under a microscope. This confirms exactly what the mole was and whether it has been fully removed. Dr. Junaid goes through the report with you and arranges any further treatment if it is needed. Lasering or burning off a mole that might be abnormal is avoided, because it destroys the tissue that needs testing.

On the face and neck, the direction and placement of each incision are planned along natural skin creases so the scar is as discreet as possible. Like any procedure, mole removal carries small risks, such as infection, bleeding, a raised or visible scar, a change in skin color and, occasionally, regrowth. Dr. Junaid will explain these before your procedure.

## Techniques and options

- **Surgical excision:** The whole mole is removed with a narrow margin and the skin is closed with fine stitches. This is the standard method for suspicious moles and allows full laboratory testing.
- **Shave removal:** A raised mole that is clearly benign is shaved level with the skin. No stitches are needed, although some color can occasionally return.
- **Snip removal:** Skin tags are numbed and snipped off at the base, often in just a few minutes.
- **Cautery:** Gentle heat seals small tags and lesions and controls any bleeding.
- **Cryotherapy:** Freezing can be used for some small skin tags and benign spots.
- **Laser removal:** For selected small, flat lesions that are clearly benign, laser may be an option Dr. Junaid recommends.

## What to expect on the day

Most removals take 15 to 30 minutes. The area is cleaned and numbed, the lesion is removed and a small dressing is applied. You go home straight away with wound-care instructions and, if a sample has gone to the laboratory, a plan for discussing the result.

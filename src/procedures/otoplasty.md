## About otoplasty

Ears that stick out are usually present from birth and are simply a variation in how the cartilage developed. Often the natural fold in the upper part of the ear has not formed fully, or the bowl of the ear is deeper than usual and pushes the ear away from the head. It is not a health problem, but it can attract unwanted comments, especially at school.

Otoplasty, sometimes called ear pinning, reshapes the cartilage so the ears sit closer to the head and look in proportion with the face. Dr. Junaid Fateh performs ear reshaping surgery in Lahore for children and adults, along with related procedures such as the repair of stretched or split earlobes.

Many adults have lived with prominent ears for most of their lives, keeping their hair long or reaching for a cap to hide them. Surgery works just as well in adulthood, and there is no upper age limit.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches otoplasty

Every ear is different, so Dr. Junaid begins by working out exactly why yours stand out. He checks the fold of the upper ear, the depth of the ear bowl, the position of the earlobe and how the two sides compare. This tells him which parts need reshaping and avoids over-correcting areas that already look fine.

He aims for ears that look natural from every angle, with soft curves rather than a flat, pressed-back appearance. Both ears are assessed together for symmetry, although no two ears are ever exactly identical, even after surgery.

For children, he takes time to speak with the child as well as the parents, explains the bandage and aftercare in simple terms, and discusses the most suitable anaesthetic. Adults are usually treated under local anaesthetic with sedation.

Like any surgery, otoplasty has risks, including bleeding, infection, thickened scars, changes in skin sensation and the ears gradually moving back a little. Dr. Junaid explains these at consultation, together with how careful technique and good aftercare help reduce them.

## Techniques and options

Depending on the shape of the ear, the plan may include:

- **Antihelical fold reshaping:** Permanent internal stitches, sometimes with gentle shaping of the cartilage, recreate the natural fold in the upper ear so it curves back softly.
- **Conchal setback or reduction:** The deep bowl of the ear is stitched back towards the head or reduced slightly, bringing the whole ear closer.
- **Earlobe correction:** Large or forward-pointing earlobes are adjusted so they match the rest of the corrected ear.
- **Asymmetry correction:** When only one ear stands out, that side is reshaped to match the other as closely as possible.
- **Torn or stretched earlobe repair:** A short procedure under local anaesthetic closes a split or enlarged piercing hole.
- **Ear reconstruction:** Rebuilds part of an ear lost to injury, or an underdeveloped ear, usually in planned stages using cartilage and skin from your own body.

## What to expect on the day

Otoplasty is usually a day-case procedure that takes one to two hours. Afterwards a padded bandage is wrapped around the head, and you or your child are usually discharged the same day. You leave with clear guidance on comfort, sleeping position and when to come back for the bandage check.

## About PRP hair therapy

PRP stands for platelet-rich plasma. A small amount of your blood is taken, spun in a centrifuge to separate the platelet-rich layer, and that concentrated plasma is injected into the thinning areas of your scalp. Platelets carry growth factors that are thought to support weak follicles, lengthen the growing phase of the hair cycle and improve the thickness of each hair.

PRP is not a substitute for a hair transplant. It cannot grow hair where follicles have already been lost for years, and it does not move hair from one place to another. Where it may help is in early to moderate thinning, when follicles are still there but producing finer, weaker hair, and as a supporting treatment around a transplant.

The research on PRP is encouraging but mixed, and the response varies a great deal from one person to the next. Some people notice less shedding and fuller hair within a few months, while others see little change. Being clear about this from the start is part of deciding whether it is worth trying.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches PRP hair therapy

Hair fall has many causes, and PRP only makes sense once the cause is understood. Dr. Junaid Fateh begins by examining your scalp, the pattern of thinning and your medical history. Sudden heavy shedding after an illness such as dengue or typhoid, after stress or after childbirth is often temporary, while problems such as low iron or an underactive thyroid need treating directly. For that reason he may ask for blood tests first.

If PRP is suitable, he explains a realistic course and how it fits with other options, such as medication now or a transplant later. Each session is prepared fresh from your own blood under sterile conditions, and the plasma is spread evenly across the thinning zones rather than placed in a few spots.

He also checks for reasons to avoid or delay treatment, including some blood or platelet disorders, blood-thinning medicines, an active scalp infection and pregnancy. Side effects are usually minor, such as tenderness, pinpoint bleeding, mild swelling or a headache for a day, and infection is rare. He goes through these with you before you decide.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Standalone PRP course:** a series of sessions around a month apart for early pattern thinning, followed by a review of how you have responded.
- **PRP with medication:** combining injections with prescribed medicines that slow pattern hair loss, as the two work in different ways.
- **PRP around a hair transplant:** sessions before or after FUE or FUT to support the existing hair and the healing scalp.
- **Maintenance sessions:** if you respond well, top-up treatments every few months to help keep the improvement.
- **PRP for thinning in women:** a gentle option for a widening parting or diffuse thinning once medical causes have been ruled out.

## What to expect on the day

A session usually takes 30–60 minutes. Blood is drawn from your arm, much like a routine test, and processed while you wait. Your scalp is cleaned and may be numbed, then a series of small injections is given across the treated area. You can return to normal activity the same day, though the scalp may feel tender for a day or so.

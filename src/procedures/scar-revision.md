## About Scar Revision

Any wound that reaches the deeper layers of the skin heals with a scar. Most fade into a fine line, but some heal wide, raised, sunken or tight, or end up running across natural skin creases where they catch the light. Scars from road accidents, burns, cuts, earlier operations or skin conditions can affect how you feel about your appearance and, in some cases, how freely you can move.

Scar revision is a group of treatments that make a scar less noticeable or less restrictive. It may involve cutting out the old scar and closing the skin more precisely, changing the direction of the scar, softening raised tissue with injections or smoothing the surface. Often more than one method is combined over time.

Dr. Junaid Fateh is a plastic, cosmetic and reconstructive surgeon who trained at Jinnah Burn & Reconstructive Surgery Centre, Lahore. Scar revision draws on both sides of his work: reconstructive skills to release and rebuild tissue, and cosmetic attention to how the final line looks.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches scar revision

At your consultation Dr. Junaid examines the scar closely, noting its width, height, color, direction and maturity, and whether it limits movement. He will ask how and when the scar formed, and whether you or your family tend to form keloids, since this changes the plan.

He then explains what is realistically achievable. A wide scar on the cheek may become a fine line. A keloid on the ear may need surgery followed by injections. A tight burn scar across a joint may need the surrounding tissue rearranged to restore movement. Natural-looking results come from careful planning of incision lines, gentle handling of tissue and well-judged stitching.

Timing matters too. Many scars keep softening on their own for up to 18 months, so operating on an immature scar can be premature. Like any surgery, scar revision carries risks, including infection, wound separation, a scar that heals no better or returns thicker, and color change. Dr. Junaid goes through the risks with you at consultation.

## Techniques and options

- **Scar excision:** The old scar is cut out and the edges are closed in layers with fine stitches to leave a neater line.
- **Z-plasty and W-plasty:** Geometric techniques that change the direction of a scar or break up a straight line so it blends into natural creases and releases tension.
- **Contracture release:** A tight scar is released and the gap is closed with nearby skin flaps or a skin graft so the area can move freely.
- **Steroid injections:** Injected into raised or keloid scars over a series of sessions to flatten and soften them.
- **Resurfacing:** Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include dermabrasion, microneedling or laser to smooth an uneven surface.
- **Fat grafting:** Your own fat can lift a sunken or tethered scar and improve the quality of the skin over it.
- **Silicone and pressure therapy:** Gels, sheets or pressure garments support healing and help stop raised scars from returning.

## What to expect on the day

Most scar revisions are done under local anesthesia as a day case and take from 30 minutes to two hours, depending on the size and number of scars. The area is cleaned and numbed, the planned technique is carried out and a dressing is applied. You go home soon afterwards with instructions on wound care, scar massage and sun protection, plus a date for stitch removal if needed.

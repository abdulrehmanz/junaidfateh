## About Skin Cancer Surgery

Skin cancer begins when skin cells grow out of control, most often after years of sun exposure. The three main types are basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma. Basal cell carcinoma grows slowly and rarely spreads, but left alone it can damage the nose, eyelid or ear. Squamous cell carcinoma can spread if neglected, and melanoma, although less common, is the most serious type.

In Pakistan, strong sunshine and years of outdoor work in fields, on building sites or on the roads all add up. Skin cancer can also arise in old burn scars or wounds that have never fully healed. Spotting a suspicious lesion early usually means a smaller operation and a smaller scar.

Surgery is the main treatment for most skin cancers. Dr. Junaid Fateh, a plastic and reconstructive surgeon working in Lahore and Layyah, removes the cancer with a safe margin of healthy skin and then closes the wound in a way that respects your appearance, which matters most on the face.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches skin cancer surgery

At consultation, Dr. Junaid examines the lesion and the rest of your skin, asks how long it has been there and whether it has changed, and checks nearby lymph nodes where relevant. If the diagnosis is uncertain, a small biopsy may come first. He then explains the likely type of cancer, the margin needed and how the wound will be repaired, all in plain language.

His first priority is removing the cancer completely. His second is a repair that looks and works well. On the face, he plans incisions along natural creases and borrows nearby skin of a similar colour and texture so the repair blends in. Around the eyelids, nose and lips, he also protects how these features work, so the eye still closes and breathing stays comfortable.

Every piece of removed tissue is sent for histopathology, and Dr. Junaid goes through the report with you. If the edges are not clear, he explains the next step. For melanoma or more advanced cancers, he may refer you to an oncologist for staging or further treatment.

Like any surgery, skin cancer surgery has risks, and results vary between people. Dr. Junaid goes through the risks with you at consultation so you can decide with confidence.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Excision biopsy:** Removing a small suspicious lesion completely so the laboratory can confirm what it is.
- **Wide local excision:** Removing a confirmed cancer with a measured margin of healthy skin around it.
- **Direct closure:** Bringing the wound edges together in a neat line, often along a natural skin crease.
- **Local flap reconstruction:** Moving neighbouring skin into the wound for a close match in colour and texture, useful on the nose, cheek and eyelids.
- **Skin graft:** Covering a larger wound with skin taken from another area, such as in front of or behind the ear.
- **Staged reconstruction:** Removing the cancer first and completing the repair once the laboratory confirms clear margins.
- **Sentinel lymph node biopsy:** A staging test used for some melanomas, discussed with you if it is relevant.
- **Mohs micrographic surgery:** The cancer is removed in thin layers that are examined under the microscope during the procedure, continuing only where cancer remains. It saves as much healthy skin as possible and is used mainly for basal and squamous cell cancers on the face and other sensitive areas.

## What to expect on the day

Most procedures are done under local anaesthetic, so you are awake but comfortable. The time needed depends on the size of the cancer and the type of repair. You go home with a dressing and written aftercare instructions. Larger repairs may need a general anaesthetic and a one-night stay.

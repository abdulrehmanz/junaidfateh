## About Skin Grafting

A skin graft is a piece of healthy skin moved from one part of your body, called the donor site, to cover a wound somewhere else. When the graft is first placed, it has no blood supply of its own. Over the next few days, tiny new vessels grow into it from the wound bed, and it becomes a living, permanent part of your skin.

Grafting is used when a wound is too large or too deep to close with stitches or to heal well on its own. Common reasons include deep burns, injuries where skin has been lost, wounds left after removing a skin cancer, chronic ulcers, and gaps created when a tight burn scar is released.

Dr. Junaid Fateh is a plastic and reconstructive surgeon who uses skin grafts as part of burn, trauma and cancer reconstruction. Choosing the right type of graft and the right donor site has a big influence on how the final result looks and feels.

## How Dr. Junaid approaches skin grafting

A graft can only take on a healthy wound, so the first step is to assess the wound bed. Dr. Junaid checks for infection, dead tissue and blood supply, and reviews conditions that slow healing, such as diabetes, smoking, anaemia and poor nutrition. Some wounds need cleaning, dressings or removal of dead tissue before grafting can succeed.

He then matches the graft to the job. For large areas, a thin split-thickness graft covers more ground. For the face, hands and other visible or mobile areas, a full-thickness graft usually gives a better colour match and tightens less. Donor sites are chosen so they are hidden by clothing or natural creases where possible.

Attention to detail in theatre matters. The graft is laid carefully, fixed with fine stitches or staples, and held firmly against the wound with a dressing so fluid cannot collect beneath it. Joints may be splinted so that movement does not disturb the graft in the first days.

Like any surgery, skin grafting carries risks, and results vary between people. Dr. Junaid goes through the risks with you at consultation and explains how you can help your graft take.

## Techniques and options

Options Dr. Junaid may recommend include:

- **Split-thickness skin graft:** A thin layer of skin, usually from the thigh, ideal for covering large wounds. It can be meshed so it stretches further.
- **Full-thickness skin graft:** The full depth of skin, usually from behind the ear, the groin or the inner upper arm, suited to the face and hands.
- **Composite graft:** A small graft of skin with fat or cartilage, for example from the ear, to rebuild the rim of the nostril.
- **Dermal substitutes:** A layer placed on the wound to build a stronger base before a thin graft is added, used in selected cases.
- **Wound bed preparation:** Cleaning and dressing the wound, sometimes over several sessions, so it is ready to accept a graft.
- **Negative pressure dressings:** A sealed dressing with gentle suction that can help the wound bed improve and the graft stay in contact, where suitable.

## What to expect on the day

Small grafts can be done under local anaesthetic, while larger ones need a general anaesthetic and a stay of one to three days. You wake with a firm dressing over the graft and a separate one on the donor site. The team explains how to keep both clean, dry and still until your first dressing change.
